Chapter 1 Introduction:  
Key Benefits of SMARTfit Training 
SMARTfit delivers exciting evidence-based gamified programming that trains the brain and body 
together to improve brain health and human performance simultaneously. Known as dual 
tasking, respected published studies have shown this to be faster and more effective than 
training each separately.   
Because all performance starts with the brain, SMARTfit benefits all populations.  It integrates 
easily into existing programming to keep a facility buzzing 24/7.  To engage, motivate, and retain 
end users, thousands of pre-designed activities and programs measure results and track 
progress in real time.  
 
SMARTfit’s unique strength is its “top-down” cognitive-motor, dual-task approach (CMDT).  In 
addition to training the brain to react faster (bottom-up cognitive processing), it also challenges 
the executive function domain of the brain to make quicker and better decisions, solve 
problems, improve working memory, enhance cognitive flexibility, build cognitive reserve, and 
more.

All this happens while performing a variety of physical movements and tasks, which carry over 
to daily life. Studies have revealed that it is this combination of reaction and executive function 
training that leads to greater transference to real-world results than simpler reaction or non-
brain-led strength or cardio training. 
In summary, to enhance human performance and rehabilitation, SMARTfit links the brain and 
body through movement, creating and strengthening neural pathways so users can: 
• 
Make quicker, smarter decisions while moving. 
• 
Execute physical and mental skills more efficiently. 
• 
Process visuals faster in order to react faster. 
• 
Train to enter the flow/zone quickly. 
• 
Improve focus, balance, coordination, and speed. 
• 
Build cognitive reserve to delay cognitive decline.

Recommended Time and Game Settings for Activities and Programs: 
Cardiorespiratory 
• 
Make the Games Last Longer - Set game times anywhere between 30 to 300 seconds. 
Longer play equals better cardio workout. In group settings, make sure each player gets 
several turns. 
• 
Move the Start Line Back - Place the start line farther from the targets. Start with 10 feet 
and go up to 30 feet or more if space allows. Running back and forth increases heart 
rate fast. 
• 
Use Team Relays - Create simple team relay races using SMARTfit. Choose a longer game 
time (up to 300 seconds) so players stay moving and rotate turns. 
• 
Keep Everyone Moving - While players wait for their turn, have them do simple 
exercises in line. For example, jumping jacks, squats, or high knees. This keeps their 
heart rate up and keeps them engaged.

Speed/Agility 
• 
Keep Games Short - Use 30 to 60 second rounds so players can go full speed without 
getting too tired. 
• 
Mix Up Movement Directions - Have players move forward, backward, and sideways to 
the targets. This builds full-body coordination and better footwork. 
• 
Sprint and Retrieve - Throw a beanbag at the targets. Players must sprint to retrieve it 
and sprint back to the start. Repeat quickly. This builds explosive speed. 
• 
Start Close for Quick Reactions - Set a short start line (about 5 feet away) for fast 
movement back and forth between the start line and the targets. 
• 
Set Multiple Start Lines - Place 2–3 start lines at different distances. Have players run to 
the target from each line like a “suicide drill.” This improves stamina and change of 
direction. 
• 
Add Footwork Tools - Use speed ladders, dots, or cones between the start line and the 
target. Players move through them before hitting the target. This builds foot speed and 
coordination. 
Sports Skill/Accuracy  
• 
Incorporate soccer balls for target drills involving foot-eye coordination, such as 
dribbling and striking the target using controlled kicks. 
• 
Use basketballs for passing or dribbling drills. Players must dribble, pass, or bounce the 
ball to hit SMARTfit targets at varying heights. 
• 
Integrate tennis balls for precision-based drills that require hand-eye coordination while 
performing underhand throws, overhand throws, or controlled bounce throws. 
Strength 
• 
Core-based drills such as holding a plank while alternating hand taps on targets are 
excellent for building core strength, shoulder stability, and endurance under fatigue. 
• 
Use overhead strikes with hand weights or dumbbells to engage the shoulders, arms, 
and upper back. 
• 
Players perform a squat, then explode upward to strike a target with their hand or a 
medicine ball. This builds lower-body strength, power, and movement coordination. 
• 
Use a light-to-moderate medicine ball for wall taps or wall passes aimed at targets. This 
improves rotational strength, explosive power, and upper-body coordination. 
Balance 
• 
Incorporate both static and dynamic balance drills to improve postural control and 
reduce fall risk. 
• 
Use standing balance variations with or without support, depending on participant 
ability. 
• 
Adjust target selection to challenge balance progressively. More targets in play require 
greater stability and control. 
• 
Static balance positions include narrow stance, staggered stance, tandem stance, and 
single-leg stance. These build ankle, knee, and hip stability. Ensure that stance difficulty 
is appropriate for the participant’s level.

Cognitive Cool Downs: Executive function and working memory 
• 
The cool-down is a vital part of the session, allowing participants to gradually lower their 
heart rate while keeping the brain actively engaged. This phase should be calming, low 
in physical intensity, and mentally stimulating. 
• 
Select cognitively demanding but low-activity SMARTfit games, such as: 
o Pattern Recognition 
o Math Equations (adjust difficulty by modifying max values) 
o Spelling (create custom word lists suited to the group) 
• 
Avoid fast paced or physically demanding drills. Focus instead on attention, memory, 
and decision-making to leave participants mentally sharp and physically relaxed. 
How to Organize Your Group for a Class on SMARTfit System 
The workouts in this manual are designed for both individual and group training sessions. For 
group training, it is essential to plan and organize the group quickly in order to get players into 
action as soon as the lesson starts.  
SMARTfit Stations can accommodate up to 10 participants per station. It is preferable to match 
participants of similar ages/abilities when choosing a team for each station. To organize teams, 
get the whole group to stand in a line ranging in height from shortest to tallest. Then count off 
the players, depending on the number of stations. If there are 4 stations, count off from the 
front to back: 1, 2, 3, 4, 1, 2, 3, 4, and so on and then send all the 1’s to stand at the start marker 
of station 1, 2’s to station 2, and so on.  Provide each team with a different colored band.  
Additional Tips 
The equipment list for each drill is per station. You can substitute as desired, using equipment 
on hand to complement the ball kit shipped with each SMARTfit™ system.

Chapter 4 
Group Training Drills 
Group Training Format 
Group classes can run between 20 and 50 minutes, depending on the number of exercise repetitions 
and stations included. Each station can accommodate up to 8 participants, regardless of age or skill 
level. Programs should be designed with modifications and progressions to ensure all participants 
can engage safely and effectively. 
Suggested Group Fitness Class Formats 
Warm-up: The Warm-Up should run for 5 minutes within a 50-minute program. The main objective 
of the warm-up is to loosen the muscles and prepare the participants for some fast-paced 
movement. A proper warm-up will prepare individuals for instruction, reduce the chance of injury, 
and set a fun mood for SMARTfit Training. 
Skill: The Skill Development section should run for about 10 minutes within a 50-minute program. 
The main focus here is the connection between the brain and body. Categories include speed, 
agility, balance, hand-eye coordination, and reaction time. Skill development transfers into the 
successful completion of life’s everyday activities. 
Cardio: The Cardiorespiratory Endurance section should run for about 15 minutes within a 50-
minute program. The main focus is cardio endurance, stamina, and calorie expenditure. Cardio 
activity enhances circulation to the brain and muscles, supporting both cognitive performance and 
physical health. 
Strength: The strength section should run for about 5 minutes within a 50-minute program. The 
main focus is on strengthening the abdominal muscles, back muscles, and pelvic floor. A strong body 
improves balance, posture, and injury resistance. This can be accomplished through exercises like 
sit-ups, overhead presses, planks, and bodyweight squats.  
Balance: The balance section should run for about 5 minutes within a 50-minute program. The main 
focus is to reduce fall risk, enhance proprioception and maintain functional independence. They 
includes static exercises like tandem, single leg, or narrow stances. Incorporate unstable surfaces 
with these exercises to demand more on the participants. 
Cool-Down: The cool-down should last 5 to 10 minutes within a 50-minute program. This allows the 
participants to lower their heart rate, encourage recovery and engage mental focus that leaves 
participants calm after their session.  For this section use higher level brain games such as Pairing, 
Equations, or Spelling which involve more thinking and less movement.

Modes of Play 
There are four primary ways to run drills on the SMARTfit system. 
For the first three modes listed below, refer to the Predesigned Activities found in the SMARTfit App 
under the Play tab for examples: 
Individual: Each player completes the entire game on their own from start to finish. Once the first 
player finishes, the next player steps up and repeats the same game. This continues until all players 
have taken their turn. 
Partners: Players pair up within their team. The first pair completes the game together until the 
timer runs out. Once time is up, the next pair starts a new game and continues until their time ends. 
Repeat until all pairs have participated. 
Team: Team members take turns during a continuous game. Each player performs a brief segment, 
such as striking 1 or more targets, then returning to the start line to tag the next player. Players may 
strike targets with their hands or use balls for throwing drills. This format allows multiple turns per 
player within a single timed session. 
* See Sections 3 and 4 of this manual for detailed examples on how to run group training classes. 
Round Robin: This mode is similar to the Team format, but with quicker rotations. Each player 
strikes only one target during their turn, then immediately peels off to the end of the line. The next 
player continues by responding to the previous strike, and so on. This style works especially well for 
ball play and encourages fast-paced engagement.

SMARTfit’s power lies in its gamified delivery of programming that will continuously demand faster and more 
accurate results from its participants without incurring overwhelm or boredom. This form of gamified practice, 
delivering score under time pressure, triggers engagement, incentive and high repetitions to create the practice 
required to develop and strengthen neural pathways. It is this intensified motivation to improve game results 
that drives the demand on the brain and motor system to develop new and more efficient neural pathways.  
The more individuals practice at a higher level than their comfort zone, the faster the entire neuro system will 
respond to meet the increasing demand. Continued practice at ever-increasing levels of demand is how 
accelerated results are achieved. Of significance, the SMARTfit problem-solving games serve to achieve the dual 
task engagement, which is the key to accelerating cognitive-motor improvement. This is how champions are 
developed in record time. 
Data provides evidence of a person’s present level of function and later proof of progress. SMARTfit’s tests are 
quick and easy to administer, with progress reporting designed to reveal the effectiveness of subsequent 
training as it applies to sports performance, activities of daily living, decreased fall risk, and other performance 
indicators while also enhancing self-confidence and engagement.

SOCCER – HIGH IMPACT  
SKILL 1 – Stationary Foot Dribble (Level 3):  
Game: Seek the Color - Seconds: 240 - Sensitivity: High - Selection: Middle 3 Rows  
Equipment: Soccer ball – Start Line: 10 – 15’ Away 
• 
Players line up in equal, diverse groups in front of each station. 
• 
The first player dribbles a soccer ball toward their assigned station. 
• 
While performing alternating toe tap drags with each foot continuously, the player strikes 
five targets using their hands. 
• 
Each player will only strike the targets matching the GO color displayed at their station. 
• 
For example, if the GO target is yellow on station 1, then all players on station 1 must only 
strike the yellow target. 
• 
After completing five successful strikes, the player passes the soccer ball to the next player 
in line and returns to the end of the line. 
• 
The next player immediately begins dribbling toward their station and repeats the same 
pattern. 
• 
Players continue this sequence until the allotted time expires.

•

pattern.

Players continue this rotation until the allotted time expires.
