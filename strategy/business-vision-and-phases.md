# Business vision and growth phases

Status: Robert's strategic direction; concept only, not a financial forecast or public promise

## Vision from Robert

Robert wants to sell SMARTfit systems online and use revenue from those sales to help fund a physical location **in or near San Antonio, Texas**. The intended concept is a combination location: demonstrate SMARTfit products, provide training and wellness activities, and potentially offer clinical services through an appropriately qualified and authorized clinical operation. Robert would also like to evaluate incorporating a Hypergravity suit, which he says is used at the Neuro Adaption Center in Oxnard, including the possibility of patients engaging with SMARTfit while wearing the suit.

The location, business model, clinical provider structure, service menu, funding schedule, demo products, and Hypergravity arrangement do not yet exist as confirmed operating facts. The combined equipment idea is a feasibility and clinical-evaluation hypothesis, not a proven treatment or established outcome. This document does not claim that a location is open, financed, affiliated with the Oxnard center, endorsed by SMARTfit, covered by insurance, or authorized to provide clinical services.

## Phased model

### Phase 1: Online product sales and buyer education

- Build a credible, all-audience website that helps institutional buyers understand SMARTfit systems and request a private sales conversation.
- Keep pricing private and quote only through an approved sales process.
- Describe only current products, terms, and sales/service arrangements that have been confirmed.
- Learn from buyer inquiries: market, organization type, product interest, space and configuration needs, demo preference, and purchase timing. Do not collect patient or other sensitive health information.
- Track actual sales and costs separately from aspirational future-center funding. Do not make a public claim that sales revenue guarantees or directly finances a specific opening date.

### Phase 2: Feasibility and funding for a San Antonio-area combination location

- Define the specific city/region and whether the operation is a demonstration showroom, training venue, wellness facility, clinical facility, or a combination. Robert's current geographic preference is in or near San Antonio.
- Decide which activities are sales demonstrations, general wellness/training, and licensed clinical care; define the responsible operator and professionals for each.
- Build a written feasibility model covering facility costs, product acquisition or demo arrangements, staffing, insurance, permits, safety, accessibility, utilization, and operating cash.
- Confirm with SMARTfit which products can be displayed, demonstrated, or purchased for the intended use and what dealer, demonstration, brand, and training permissions apply.
- Secure the site, funding, operating plan, and required approvals before describing the location as available.

### Phase 3: Equip and open the demo location

- Select systems from the current, orderable catalog according to buyer demand and confirmed demo permissions, rather than promising every product before cost and availability are known.
- Establish visitor scheduling, safety screening appropriate to the approved activity, equipment supervision, staff training, accessibility, incident procedures, and privacy practices.
- Publish location details and in-person demo booking only after the site is operational and services, hours, fees, and responsibilities are confirmed.

### Phase 4: Evaluate Hypergravity integration

- Identify the exact Hypergravity product, manufacturer, intended use, training requirements, costs, and legal/insurance considerations.
- Confirm purchasing or licensing rights, product availability, safety procedures, staff qualifications, and any restrictions on the name, logo, images, or claims.
- Verify the exact name and spelling of the Neuro Adaption Center in Oxnard and obtain permission before referencing its use, staff, methods, or outcomes in public materials. Do not imply partnership, endorsement, or transferability of its program.
- Evaluate the proposed simultaneous use of Hypergravity and SMARTfit as a separate feasibility question with manufacturer input, qualified clinician review, safety protocols, and appropriate prospective outcome evaluation. Do not promise patient outcomes or reimbursement.
- Decide whether Hypergravity is a separate demo offering, part of a broader program, or outside the initial location scope. Do not imply it is a SMARTfit product.
- Keep any medical or rehabilitation service model separate from equipment demonstrations and obtain appropriate professional, regulatory, and legal guidance before offering clinical care.

## Website implications now

- The initial site serves online product sales and buyer education; it must not claim there is a showroom or in-person demo site.
- A future location may become a distinct conversion path, with its own location, access, demo-scheduling, safety, and service information once it exists.
- Capture buyer interest in an in-person demo as an optional research signal, but tell buyers that availability is not yet established. Do not offer a location appointment before one is open.
- Keep SMARTfit product facts, Hypergravity facts, and any future service/program outcomes in separate source and claims records.
- Never imply SMARTfit or the Neuro Adaption Center sponsors, endorses, or partners in the future venture without written permission.

## Decisions to make

1. What specific city/region in or near San Antonio should the future center serve, and what site criteria matter?
2. Which combination of showroom/demo, training, wellness, and licensed clinical operations will be in scope at opening?
3. Which SMARTfit products are essential for demonstrations, and what purchase/demo permissions and budget apply?
4. What is the exact Hypergravity product and manufacturer, and may it be safely and appropriately used at the same time as a SMARTfit system?
5. What evidence, clinician-led protocol, safety controls, and evaluation would be required before making any combined-use outcome claim?
6. What services, if any, would Robert personally provide versus SMARTfit or other qualified providers?
7. What measurable online-sales, cash-reserve, financing, or other milestones would make signing a lease financially responsible?
8. What staffing, credentials, insurance, accessibility, safety, privacy, billing, and permitting requirements apply to the chosen operating model?

## Planning guardrails

- Treat projected sales as uncertain; do not make a lease or capital decision using gross sales alone.
- Model product margins, commissions, taxes, advertising, returns, customer-acquisition cost, fulfillment, and operating expenses before estimating funds available for a location.
- Avoid clinical, safety, rehabilitation, performance, or health-outcome promises unless the service model, evidence, qualifications, and review support the specific wording.
- Keep this vision internal until Robert approves public positioning and the operational facts are true.
