# NeuroAdaption Center website review

Review date: October 27, 2026  
Source: [oxnardphysicaltherapy.com](https://oxnardphysicaltherapy.com/)  
Status: public-site content review; not a medical, legal, regulatory, billing, or technical audit

## Coverage and method

- Retrieved the public XML sitemap and enumerated 58 URLs: homepage (1), informational/conversion pages (10), blog/resource pages (9), service pages (27), and location pages (11).
- All 58 sitemap URLs returned HTTP 200 in the page crawl. Main-page content and the HyperGravity, professional referral, contact, and testimonial pages were also fetched for closer review.
- Extracted page titles, headings, and visible text; reviewed site-wide messaging, repeated medical/outcome/coverage claims, calls to action, and footer disclosures.
- The sitemap's shared `lastmod` date is December 2, 2025. This is a snapshot of the public, sitemap-listed site, not a guarantee that every unpublished, excluded, or dynamically loaded page was included.
- SMARTfit sales and specification materials, independent clinical evidence, device regulatory records, payer policies, and NeuroAdaption's underlying claims were not independently verified as part of this site review.

## High-level read

The site is structured as a condition-specific lead-generation funnel around neurological and neuropathy services, with HyperGravity and Neurogenx positioned as treatment technologies. It contains broad service, condition, testimonial, referral, contact, blog, and local-area content. Its layout and lead-capture patterns may be useful as an example for a future center, but its medical claims, provider identity, reimbursement language, and local SEO model should not be copied without separate verification and adaptation to the actual San Antonio-area business.

The footer repeatedly identifies “Ventura County PT Guide” as a healthcare directory/referral service, says it is not a medical provider, and discloses that featured providers may compensate it for referrals. Elsewhere, pages speak as if NeuroAdaption Center itself treats patients, accepts Medicare, conducts evaluations, provides treatment, and offers the only HyperGravity location. Resolve this identity/provider-role difference before modeling a future site's structure or claims on the reference.

## Relevant content patterns observed

- Homepage, service hub, HyperGravity, condition pages, and professional-referral page use strong outcome and superiority wording, including “immediate results,” large outcome percentages, “world's only,” and comparisons with traditional PT.
- HyperGravity pages describe the device as patented/FDA-cleared, say it treats multiple conditions, and attribute measurable changes to sessions. The precise regulatory status, intended use, study basis, device manufacturer, and substantiation were not verified.
- The professional page describes a proposed mechanism and asserts per-session improvement and Medicare Part B coverage for HyperGravity therapy. The site footer separately cautions that payer coverage varies by provider and plan. These are claims appearing on the reference site, not confirmation that the combination or device is covered.
- The homepage and service pages state that Medicare and major insurance are accepted, while the contact page asks people to verify individual benefits. Coverage language varies by page and should not be treated as a blanket guarantee.
- Testimonials and clinician endorsements are prominent. Their presence does not independently establish typical outcomes; credentials, consent, attribution, and permission would require verification before reuse.
- Many condition and location pages repeat strong claims and location-specific targeting. For San Antonio, build only location/service pages that reflect a real operating facility and genuine service footprint.

## Medicare distinction

Medicare.gov states that Part B helps cover **medically necessary outpatient physical therapy** for eligible people, with provider certification and cost-sharing rules. This general coverage statement does not establish that:

1. HyperGravity as a device or named modality is separately covered;
2. SMARTfit use during therapy is separately covered or billable;
3. the combined protocol is covered, medically necessary, or payable;
4. a future San Antonio business or its staff would be eligible to furnish or bill the service; or
5. a commercial plan covers the same service.

Any future clinical model and insurance claim must be reviewed against the actual provider enrollment, clinician qualifications, documented plan of care, billed skilled service, place of service, payer policy, and applicable coding/compliance advice. Do not market the combined equipment experience as “covered by Medicare” or promise reimbursement based on the NeuroAdaption website.

Source: [Medicare.gov, Physical therapy services](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/physical-therapy-services), reviewed October 27, 2026.

## SMARTfit + Hypergravity idea

The proposed concept is understandable: a clinician might explore whether interactive SMARTfit tasks can be paired with movement performed while wearing a Hypergravity suit, potentially combining physical and cognitive-motor demands in one supervised activity. That is a **hypothesis for feasibility and clinical evaluation**, not an established combined treatment protocol or evidence of “amazing results.”

Before any patient-facing or outcome claim, establish:

- exact suit model/manufacturer and current instructions, intended use, indications, contraindications, and safety requirements;
- SMARTfit model, mounting/configuration, software/license, and permitted intended use;
- written manufacturer approval or guidance for simultaneous use and any setup/adaptation needed;
- review by qualified physical therapists and the responsible medical/clinical director, including patient selection, fall risk, assistance, emergency procedures, and monitoring;
- a prospective, ethically reviewed evaluation plan with prespecified outcomes, adverse-event monitoring, and an appropriate comparison if effectiveness is to be claimed;
- consent and privacy protections if collecting patient feedback, images, testimonials, or outcomes; and
- separate reimbursement review for the actual skilled service rather than the equipment combination.

Patient feedback can help assess acceptability and experience, but testimonials alone do not prove safety, effectiveness, generalizability, or coverage.
