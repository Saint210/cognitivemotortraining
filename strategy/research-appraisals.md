# SMARTfit research and white-paper appraisal

Status: internal evidence review; not approved public copy  
Reviewed: 27 October 2026

## Scope and method

This is a document-by-document appraisal of the 12 research, white-paper, and background PDFs currently indexed in the source library. It separates studies that actually used SMARTfit from general dual-task/exergaming research, product marketing, and records of planned research. The appraisal is a structured first-pass review of the supplied documents and cited primary sources, not a systematic review, meta-analysis, clinical guideline, or formal risk-of-bias assessment.

The page numbers below refer to the PDF pages in the local source collection. The extracted text is a navigation aid; consult the original PDF before quoting or publishing. A study's findings apply to its specific population, intervention, comparator, and outcomes only. SMARTfit use in a study does not by itself establish the product's efficacy, superiority, safety for a particular patient, or a clinical indication.

## Direct SMARTfit use in research

### 1. Buffalo community-based MCI pilot

- **Citation:** Jhaveri S, Romanyk M, Glatt R, Satchidanand N. “SMARTfit Dual-Task Exercise Improves Cognition and Physical Function in Older Adults With Mild Cognitive Impairment: Results of a Community-Based Pilot Study.” *Journal of Aging and Physical Activity*. 2023;31(4):621–632. DOI: [10.1123/japa.2022-0040](https://doi.org/10.1123/japa.2022-0040).
- **Source:** [Buffalo-Study.pdf](../source-library/smartfit/research-and-white-papers/Buffalo-Study.pdf), especially PDF pp. 1, 3–9.
- **Type and methods:** Community-based, one-group pre/post pilot. Ten adults with MCI (mean age 73; all self-identified as African American) received a 60-minute, instructor-led SMARTfit dual-task session weekly for 12 weeks at one YMCA. Eight of ten completed all sessions; all completed at least 11. There was no control group or randomization. Four participants from the eight-person activity-development phase also took part in the impact phase.
- **Reported findings:** Median Trail Making Test A/B completion times, several Stroop outcomes, and total Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB) score improved from baseline to endpoint. SPPB walking score did not significantly change. These are within-group pre/post changes, not estimates of the effect compared with usual care or another program.
- **Funding and support:** The paper reports NIH/NCATS and Health Foundation for Western and Central New York support. Acknowledgments state that SMARTfit staff provided programmatic and technical support. The report does not make the intervention manufacturer-independent.
- **Important limitations:** Very small, single-site, one-group pre/post sample; no control for practice, attention, social contact, or other changes; limited covariate and clinical data; no measured exercise intensity; relatively high baseline SPPB scores and a possible ceiling effect; uncertainty about interpretation of the derived Stroop interference score; limited generalizability. The authors explicitly call for larger randomized controlled trials and say this pilot cannot establish causation.
- **What it supports:** A cautious account that a small community program delivered the intervention with high attendance and observed favorable within-group changes on selected measures.
- **What it does not establish:** That SMARTfit caused the changes, prevents falls/dementia, slows MCI, improves independent living, or is more effective than exercise or cognitive training alone.
- **Safe-use decision:** May be summarized only as a preliminary, uncontrolled pilot with its sample and limitations alongside the results. Do not turn the title's “improves” into a causal marketing claim.

### 2. V-MAP preschool intervention using SMARTfit Play Pods

- **Citation:** Gu X, Moss S, Zhang X, Zhang T, Greer TL. “Effect of the Virtual Reality-Infused Movement and Activity Program (V-MAP) on Physical Activity and Cognition in Head Start Preschoolers.” *Children*. 2025;12(9):1228. DOI: [10.3390/children12091228](https://doi.org/10.3390/children12091228).
- **Source:** [SMARTfit Strike Pods- Effect of the Virtual Reality-Infused Movement and Activity Program.pdf](../source-library/smartfit/research-and-white-papers/SMARTfit%20Strike%20Pods-%20Effect%20of%20the%20Virtual%20Reality-Infused%20Movement%20and%20Activity%20Program.pdf), PDF pp. 1–13.
- **Type and methods:** Small, uncontrolled repeated-measures intervention with a one-month follow-up. Thirteen children were recruited at a North Texas Head Start center; one moved before the intervention, leaving 12 who began. The six-week program provided eight 30-minute sessions using SMARTfit Play Pods, games, and fundamental movement skills. Physical activity and selected cognitive measures were assessed before, after, and one month after the program.
- **Reported findings:** The authors report a significant increase in vigorous physical activity (VPA); moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) and sedentary behavior did not significantly change. The measured cognitive outcomes did not significantly improve immediately after the intervention. Some favorable cognitive changes appeared at one-month follow-up, but these were preliminary/non-significant trends and require confirmation. The paper reports an average of 1,105 steps per 30-minute session.
- **Funding and conflicts:** Funding came from the University of Texas at Arlington Research Enhancement Program. The authors declare no conflicts of interest.
- **Important limitations:** Small sample from one center, no control group, short intervention and follow-up, high variability in children's cognitive scores, and limited generalizability. Without a control group, maturation, test practice, or other changes cannot be ruled out. The paper itself calls the results preliminary and recommends larger randomized controlled designs.
- **Product boundary:** The intervention used SMARTfit Play Pods as described in this paper. Do not silently relabel the system as a current Gen 3 Strike Pod kit or assume the hardware/configuration is identical.
- **What it supports:** A preliminary account of this specific recess-based V-MAP program and its observed activity changes in this small sample.
- **What it does not establish:** A general cognitive benefit, improved academic achievement or school readiness, clinical efficacy, or the effectiveness of every SMARTfit system or pod configuration.
- **Safe-use decision:** May be summarized with the no-control design, sample, and non-significant immediate cognitive outcomes prominently stated. Do not claim a proven or durable cognitive effect.

### 3. Parkinson's disease trial registry

- **Record:** ClinicalTrials.gov [NCT03921359](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03921359), “Effect of SMARTfit Training on Motor, Cognitive Functions and Brain Connectivity in Individuals With Parkinson's Disease: a Pilot Study.”
- **Source:** [SMARTfit Training for Parkinson_s Disease.pdf](../source-library/smartfit/research-and-white-papers/SMARTfit%20Training%20for%20Parkinson_s%20Disease.pdf), supplied registry snapshot, PDF pp. 1–19; current record API: [ClinicalTrials.gov API record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT03921359).
- **Type and methods:** Registry record, not a peer-reviewed results paper. It describes a planned randomized, open-label crossover pilot comparing eight weeks of SMARTfit training with conventional physical training. Planned enrollment was 10; the registry reports actual enrollment of 9.
- **Status/results:** The supplied registry snapshot says the study was terminated because of COVID-19 restrictions. The current ClinicalTrials.gov record was checked during this review; it remains marked terminated and does not provide a results section. No intervention outcome can be inferred from this registration.
- **Sponsor:** University of Southern California; University of California, Irvine is listed as a collaborator in the supplied record.
- **Important limitations:** A registry documents planned research, not observed benefit. The small planned sample and early termination further preclude conclusions from the record.
- **Safe-use decision:** It is accurate to describe this as a registered, terminated pilot with no results posted in the record reviewed. Check the live record again before making a time-sensitive status statement.

### 4. Acute exercise and affective inhibition conference abstract

- **Citation:** Mücke M, Greco A, Müller T, Gerber M, Pühse U, Ludyga S. “Physical but not cognitive demand of an exercise bout influences subsequent affective inhibition.” *Current Issues in Sport Science*. 2023;8(2):036. DOI: [10.36950/2023.2ciss036](https://doi.org/10.36950/2023.2ciss036).
- **Source:** [Physical but not cognitive demand…pdf](../source-library/smartfit/external-background-research-not-smartfit-validation/Physical%20but%20not%20cognitive%20demand%20of%20an%20exercise%20bout%20influences%20subsequent%20affective%20inhibition%20_%20Current%20Issues%20in%20Sport%20Science%20%28CISS%29.pdf), PDF pp. 1–3.
- **Type and methods:** Conference-book abstract describing a randomized-order, within-participant experiment. Thirty-five children aged 9–13 completed four 10-minute exercise sessions on a SMARTfit Single, varying physical effort and cognitive load.
- **Reported findings:** Lower physical effort was associated with greater improvement in reaction time on the affective Stroop task; no corresponding physical-effort effect appeared on the standard Stroop task. The abstract reports no main effect of cognitive load and no interaction.
- **Important limitations:** The available source is a conference abstract, not a full methods/results article; it studies acute post-session task performance, not long-term training or clinical outcomes. It does not compare SMARTfit against another product or establish product efficacy.
- **Safe-use decision:** This is a research use of SMARTfit, not merely general exercise research. It may be cited narrowly for the reported experiment, with the abstract-only and acute-outcome caveats. Do not claim SMARTfit improved general cognition or academic performance.

### 5. SMARTfit as a comparator in a Rezzil validation study

- **Citation:** Polechoński J, Horbacz A. “Assessment of the Validity and Reliability of Reaction Speed Measurements Using the Rezzil Player Application in Virtual Reality.” *Multimodal Technologies and Interaction*. 2025;9(9):91. DOI: [10.3390/mti9090091](https://doi.org/10.3390/mti9090091).
- **Source:** [Assessment_of_the_Validity_and_Reliability_of_Reac.pdf](../source-library/smartfit/external-background-research-not-smartfit-validation/Assessment_of_the_Validity_and_Reliability_of_Reac.pdf), PDF pp. 1–7 for abstract and methods/results.
- **Type and methods:** Measurement-comparison study with 43 physically fit university students. Participants completed repeated reaction tasks in Rezzil Player VR and on a SMARTfit Mini real-world panel; target hits and response speed were compared.
- **Reported findings:** The paper reports correlations between the VR and SMARTfit measures (hits: Spearman r = 0.610; average reaction speed: r = 0.535; both p < .001) and high within-test ICCs for both conditions.
- **Important limitations:** SMARTfit is the real-world comparison instrument, not the tested treatment. The comparison does not establish SMARTfit training efficacy or validate SMARTfit against an independent clinical gold standard. The young, physically fit sample limits generalization to clinical groups.
- **Safe-use decision:** It can be cited as a Rezzil-versus-SMARTfit measurement comparison, not as evidence that SMARTfit improves reaction time or patient outcomes.

## Manufacturer-originated overviews and white papers

### 6. SMARTfit and Active Aging

- **Source:** [SMARTfit and Active Aging.pdf](../source-library/smartfit/research-and-white-papers/SMARTfit%20and%20Active%20Aging.pdf), 9 pages.
- **Classification:** Undated manufacturer-originated white paper. It discusses dual-task and exercise research and describes SMARTfit as an application, but it does not report a SMARTfit participant sample, comparator, outcome dataset, or product trial.
- **Appraisal:** This is a secondary, promotional explanation of general mechanisms and cited literature. Statements about SMARTfit's potential are not product-specific clinical findings; source citations should be checked independently and attributed to their original studies.
- **Safe-use decision:** Use only as evidence of how SMARTfit describes its approach, not as evidence of clinical outcomes or efficacy.

### 7. SMARTfit in Mental Health

- **Source:** [SMARTfit in Mental Health.pdf](../source-library/smartfit/research-and-white-papers/SMARTfit%20in%20Mental%20Health.pdf), PDF pp. 1–11.
- **Classification:** SMARTfit Inc. © 2021 product white paper; no study methods, enrolled sample, control group, or SMARTfit-specific outcome analysis are presented.
- **Appraisal:** The paper discusses general exercise, mood, and neurochemical concepts, then applies them to SMARTfit. Product assertions—including mood, adherence, aggression, or suspension outcomes—are not supported in this document by a SMARTfit-controlled study. The references include general literature and a case-study link; these do not establish that SMARTfit caused a reported outcome.
- **Safe-use decision:** Do not use it to claim SMARTfit treats depression/anxiety, changes neurotransmitters, reduces aggression, or improves school discipline. Attribute product statements to the manufacturer if needed and obtain primary evidence before making outcome claims.

### 8–9. Two SMARTfit-sponsored youth overview PDFs

- **Sources:** [Youth overview PDF](../source-library/smartfit/research-and-white-papers/Enhancing%20Cognition%20and%20Academic%20Performance%20in%20Youth%20Through%20Exergaming%20and%20Dual-Task%20Training%20By-%20Ryan%20Glatt,%20MS,%20CPT,%20PhD%28c%29.pdf) and [second youth PDF variant](../source-library/smartfit/research-and-white-papers/Enhancing%20Cognition%20and%20Academic%20Performance%20in%20Youth%20Through%20Exergaming%20and%20Dual-Task%20Training%20By-%20Ryan%20Glatt,%20MS,%20CPT,%20PhD%28c%29-DDD2B292.pdf), 4 pages each.
- **Classification:** The first page identifies Ryan Glatt and SMARTfit Inc. sponsorship. The two PDFs have different file checksums, but their extracted text is identical in this source set; retain both source records and do not assume which file version is authoritative.
- **Appraisal:** These are concise narrative summaries of third-party exergaming and dual-task studies, not SMARTfit trials or systematic reviews with a documented search/appraisal method. The cited interventions, devices, populations, and academic outcomes vary. The overview's broad claims cannot be attributed to SMARTfit hardware or programs merely because SMARTfit sponsored the document.
- **Safe-use decision:** Can be identified as a SMARTfit-sponsored educational overview. For any research or outcome claim, use the original cited study and report its population and intervention; do not state that SMARTfit improves grades or cognition on this basis.

### 10. SMARTfit-sponsored military overview

- **Source:** [Enhancing Military Performance.pdf](../source-library/smartfit/research-and-white-papers/Enhancing%20Military%20Performance-%20The%20Benefits%20of%20Dual-Task%20Training%20in%20Military%20Personnel%20By-%20Ryan%20Glatt,%20MS,%20CPT,%20PhD%28c%29%20Sponsored%20by-%20SMARTfit%20Inc.pdf), 4 pages.
- **Classification:** SMARTfit-sponsored narrative overview, with general dual-task references. It does not report a military cohort trained on SMARTfit, a comparator, or SMARTfit-specific outcomes.
- **Appraisal:** The cited research may support general discussion of dual-task demands, but the paper's proposed military benefits are extrapolations, not measured SMARTfit effects in service members.
- **Safe-use decision:** Do not claim operational readiness, TBI recovery, stress resilience, or military performance benefits from SMARTfit based on this overview.

### 11. Athletic performance overview

- **Source:** [Enhancing Athletic Performance.pdf](../source-library/smartfit/research-and-white-papers/Enhancing%20Athletic%20Performance-%20The%20Neuroscience%20of%20Cognitive%20and%20Agility%20Training%20By-%20Ryan%20Glatt,%20MS,%20CPT,%20PhD%28c%29%20Credit%20to-%20SwitchedOn%20Training%20App.pdf), 4 pages.
- **Classification:** Undated narrative overview credited to the SwitchedOn Training App. No SMARTfit-specific intervention or product data are presented.
- **Appraisal:** It summarizes general sports-cognition and agility concepts and cited literature. It is not a primary study and does not demonstrate transfer from SMARTfit exercises to sports performance.
- **Safe-use decision:** Do not present as SMARTfit research or claim transfer to competitive performance without product-specific evidence.

### 12. FitBrain implementation paper

- **Citation:** Glatt RM, Patis C, Miller KJ, Merrill DA, Stubbs B, Adcock M, Giannouli E, Siddarth P. “The ‘FitBrain’ program: implementing exergaming & dual-task exercise programs in outpatient clinical settings.” *Frontiers in Sports and Active Living*. 2024;6:1449699. DOI: [10.3389/fspor.2024.1449699](https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2024.1449699).
- **Source:** [The FitBrain Program.pdf](../source-library/smartfit/research-and-white-papers/The%20_FitBrain%20Program-%20Implementing%20exergaming%20and%20dual-task%20exercise%20programs%20in%20outpatient%20clinical%20settings.pdf), PDF pp. 1–14.
- **Type and methods:** Peer-reviewed implementation/methods paper describing an outpatient program that combines multiple technologies and interventions. SMARTfit appears as one of several example tools and in sample session structures. The paper does not report a controlled evaluation or patient outcome dataset for FitBrain or SMARTfit.
- **Funding and disclosures:** Authors report no financial support for the research, authorship, or publication. The acknowledgments describe equipment support, including companies providing in-kind equipment. Ryan Glatt reports consulting/advisory relationships with multiple companies mentioned and in-kind equipment provision.
- **Limitations and safe use:** The authors identify the lack of feasibility/effectiveness data and cost/scalability concerns; they call for more robust randomized trials. It may inform implementation concepts, but it is not evidence that SMARTfit or the FitBrain program improves clinical outcomes.

## Synthesis and publication guardrails

1. The source set contains two peer-reviewed studies of interventions that directly used SMARTfit systems (the Buffalo MCI pilot and the V-MAP preschool intervention), plus a SMARTfit-use conference abstract and a measurement study in which SMARTfit was a comparator. None of these four documents establishes broad clinical efficacy, superiority, or guaranteed transfer.
2. The Buffalo paper and V-MAP intervention report promising within-sample observations, but each lacks a control group. Their small samples, population specificity, and designs do not support causal marketing claims.
3. The Parkinson's registry is not a completed results paper. Do not imply that the planned Parkinson's study found a benefit.
4. Manufacturer-sponsored overviews and white papers are useful records of messaging and hypotheses, not independent product trials. Attribute their claims and verify every substantive assertion against the original research.
5. General exercise, exergaming, and dual-task findings must not be rewritten as SMARTfit-specific evidence. Link to the original study and preserve its exact population, intervention, comparator, outcomes, and limitations.
6. Avoid treatment, prevention, diagnosis, fall-reduction, academic-achievement, operational-readiness, and mental-health efficacy claims unless supported by appropriate product-specific evidence and reviewed for the intended audience.
