## What the guide is designed to do

This is a combined category-education, business-development, and product-selection guide—not a neutral clinical evidence review. It:

1. Introduces a broad "Brain Health" market concept and cognitive-motor dual-task training (pp. 5-8, 16-18).
2. Positions SMARTfit as an anchor for a facility-wide program and describes population-specific room layouts and group formats (pp. 11-15).
3. Uses expert/customer testimonials and references to university-associated pilots for credibility (pp. 9-10, 24).
4. Explains SMARTfit hardware, software, programming, tests, data features, group training, and license options (pp. 18-39).
5. Presents implementation, business-model, ROI, training, and support themes (pp. 7-8, 35-40).
6. Routes readers to SMARTfit product, research, training, and contact resources (pp. 39-41).

## Strategic value for Robert's future site

The guide reveals the manufacturer's sales narrative and the questions buyers may bring to a consultation: who a program serves, what space and group sizes it requires, how systems differ, what programming and licenses include, what training is offered, how data is handled, and how an organization evaluates its business case.

Use those themes to create an independent, buyer-first information experience for all relevant audiences:

- A practical guide to cognitive-motor / dual-task training that distinguishes general concepts from SMARTfit-specific evidence.
- Audience-specific planning pages that start with the organization's setting, participants, staffing, space, and goals.
- A transparent system-selection guide and sourced specifications comparison.
- A readiness checklist for facility space, supervision, accessibility, connectivity, training, and procurement.
- A software, assessment, reporting, and data-questions guide that avoids unsupported diagnostic, validation, privacy, or compliance claims.
- A candid buyer's guide to program economics that helps organizations build their own assumptions instead of promising ROI.
- Research explainers that link to the original paper and state exactly what it did and did not establish.

Robert's distinct contribution should be practical sales experience, clear system navigation, transparent sourcing, and helpful consultation—not a claim to clinical authority without appropriate qualifications or independent expert review.

## Lead-magnet strategy

Robert confirms permission to offer the SMARTfit PDF as a lead magnet and that the included demo access is intended for prospective users. Preserve the lead magnet intact so prospects can use the provided demo. Do not imply that it is Robert's original guide or rebrand it without separate authorization.

Permission to offer the guide does not necessarily establish permission to edit, rebrand, excerpt, or change the PDF; keep the source document intact unless those uses are also authorized. If the planned use involves edits or rebranding, confirm that scope separately. A separate original guide can be developed with distinct copy, design, citations, and a clearly stated relationship to SMARTfit.

For an original guide, prioritize a useful decision outcome over a lengthy company pitch: help each audience define its goals, facility constraints, selection criteria, evidence questions, and next steps. Any product recommendation should point to current approved specifications; any research statement should point to the original study.

## Source-of-truth boundary

The PDF and its page-marked extraction are valuable manufacturer-source references. Candidate product facts may be checked against this guide and current specifications, with exact page/version recorded in the claim register. Research claims require the original research source. No claim is approved merely because SMARTfit included it in this sales guide.
